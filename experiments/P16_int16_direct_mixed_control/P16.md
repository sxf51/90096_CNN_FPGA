## 6. Interpretation / 解读

A complete signed16x16 product needs32 bits. Nonoverlapping two-product fields need at least gap32, making the coefficient port48 bits, beyond DSP48E2's27-bit input; splitting a coefficient alone leaves8x16 products needing24 bits and a32-bit packed port. P14 therefore splits both operands. P15 explicitly pays fabric recovery for overlapping fields. Count every DSP/fabric operation, compare P13/P16 controls, and retain failures or missed II without changing numerical gates.

Two INT8 products share one DSP only when operand packing and signed-low borrowing are handled explicitly. Exact ten-bit coefficient residuals add fabric control/adders, and adjacent-pixel Conv3 needs a5x6 window plus odd-column disposal. Complete INT16 products require separately counted limb multipliers or overlapping-field fabric recovery; compare P13-P16's matched controls. Extra multiplier/pipeline depth is paid on every pixel in nested fold schedules. PE cycle counts cannot be interpreted as network-frame times.

Explicit explanation of deviations above20%:

One complete16x16 product is bound to DSP and the other to fabric. Fabric LUT/carry/register mapping depends on operand width and clock; it is the matched control for overlapping-field repair, not a packed primitive.

LUT/FF paper budgets reserve mapping and pipeline margin. Parsed top DSP/LUT/FF, actual clock and pack16_terms iteration depth quantify the actual cost; no whole-network frame speedup is inferred from these64-term PE calls.

The matched direct controls export their arrays as ap_memory ports instead of allocating internal input/output memories. Their multiplier instances report zero external FF, leaving only loop/control registers. The paper FF allowance assumed additional external datapath stages, so it overestimates the implemented single-cycle operator schedule. Expression and Multiplexer rows account for the remaining control LUT; the mixed control's Instance row also includes its fabric multiplier.
